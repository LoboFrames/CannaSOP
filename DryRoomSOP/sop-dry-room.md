# SOP — Dry Room Operation & Debucking Readiness

**Department:** Post-Harvest · **Applies to:** Dry Room, all harvested rooms
**Cycle:** Harvest Monday · Target takedown the following Monday (Day 8) · Outer bound Day 12 (Friday)
**Downstream:** ESG / NLT Post-Processing SOP, Stage 1 (Bucking)

---

## 1. Purpose

Defines the drying environment, the daily verification routine, and the readiness criteria that authorize takedown and debucking. Delivers flower to the trim room at a moisture level the Pre-Trim Cure can finish — on the standard eight-day schedule where the load allows it, and on a controlled extension where it does not.

## 2. How the schedule works

**Day 8 is the target, not the deadline.** Harvest Monday, take down the following Monday whenever the batch passes the readiness gate. Consistency is worth real money downstream — the trim room, Rob's machine schedule, and the aunties all plan around a Monday handoff.

**Day 12 is the outer bound.** Some batches will not be ready on Day 8, and forcing them down wet is worse than extending. Drying rate is set by the water load in the room against the capacity to remove it, and both vary: plant count, wet weight, how much leaf came in, whether the room was wet-trimmed, how many portable dehumidifiers are running, and whether AC control is holding. Days 9–12 exist for that case, under defined conditions and a daily re-check. **Day 12 is not a forced takedown.** It is the point where the call stops being the technician's and becomes a documented decision by the Lead and the Supervisor.

**Most of that is knowable on Day 1.** Plant count, poundage, and leaf load are recorded at harvest. Dehumidifier count and AC control are known before the room is loaded. That is why the load assessment in §6 comes before the cycle starts rather than after it fails — a heavy load should be predicted at intake, not discovered on Monday morning.

**The target is a window, not a point.** The Pre-Trim Cure downstream (1–3 days, moisture-driven) absorbs the remainder. Flower on the wet side of the window gets a full 3-day cure; flower on target gets 1–2 days. The dry room does not need to land the final number — it needs to land inside the cure's entry window and hand off an accurate reading.

## 4. Scope & Responsibilities

**Scope:** Room preparation, load assessment, hang, environmental control across the cycle, daily monitoring, readiness verification, extension management, and handoff to bucking. Does not cover harvest/cut-down, bucking, or anything downstream of the trim room door.

- **Cultivation Technician** — sets and verifies setpoints, performs daily checks, takes branch weight and meter readings, records the log.
- **Cultivation Lead** — reviews the Day 5 checkpoint, makes the Day 7 routing call, authorizes each day of extension, decides on any batch that reaches Day 12 without passing.
- **Supervisor (Jeff — Uncle Han Sup)** — final sign-off on the completed cycle.

## 5. Safety & PPE

- Gloves, hair covering, and clean garments. The dry room is a mold-risk environment — treat entry like entry to a clean area.
- Sanitize hands and any tools before entering. Do not carry material from a room with suspected botrytis into a clean dry room.
- Watch for slip hazards around dehumidifier drain lines and condensate.
- Keep work lighting use to the minimum time needed. The room runs dark.
- Do not stand under hanging racks while adjusting them.
- **Mold scouting frequency doubles on any day past Day 8.** Extended dry time at moderate humidity is when botrytis gets its chance.

## 6. Load Assessment — Day 1, at intake

Recorded before or as the room is loaded. This is what sets the expected track.

**Record:**

- Number of plants and source rooms
- Estimated wet weight (lb)
- Leaf load — whole-plant / leaf-on, or wet-trimmed
- Number of portable dehumidifiers running, and their condition
- AC control status — holding, or known marginal
- Hang density — full room, or partial

**Classify:**

| Track | Indicators | Expected takedown |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | Typical poundage for the room, dehumidification at full complement, AC holding, normal hang density | Day 8 (Mon) |
| **Heavy** | High poundage, heavy leaf load, a dehumidifier down, marginal AC control, or a fully packed room | Day 9–11, possibly Day 12 |

**A Heavy classification is communicated at intake, not at takedown.** Tell the trim room lead and Rob on Day 1 that this batch is tracking long. They schedule a week out; a Monday morning surprise costs the whole downstream chain a day.

> **Room availability conflict.** Harvest runs weekly. An extension into Days 9–12 overlaps the next harvest's Days 1–5. A Heavy classification on Day 1 is therefore also the earliest warning that next Monday's harvest may need somewhere else to hang, or may need to be split or reduced. Raise it with the Cultivation Lead at intake. See Open Items.

## 7. Equipment & Materials

TrolMaster controller and environmental sensors · orange handheld temperature monitor (calibration reference) · portable dehumidifiers · floor fans · fresh air intake and exhaust · **FLIR MR40 pin-type moisture meter — the room's dedicated unit, see §8** · spare MR40 pin set · 70% IPA and wipes for meter pins · hanging scale · branch tags · room hygrometer · dry room log.

## 8. Room Prep — before flower goes in

1. Sanitize the room. Confirm no residue or debris from the prior batch.
2. Confirm every portable dehumidifier drains freely and its filter is clean. **Count them and record the number** — this feeds the load assessment.
3. **Verify TrolMaster sensor calibration against the orange handheld before the room is loaded**, not after. A sensor reading 5% low will silently overdry the entire batch.
4. **Confirm the dry room's own moisture meter is the one in hand.** The room runs a single dedicated FLIR MR40, labelled for this room. Check that the pins are straight and clean, and fit a fresh pin set if either is bent or corroded. **Do not substitute another meter mid-cycle.** No pin meter sold is calibrated for cannabis — every one of them reads a wood scale — so the 11–12% acceptance band is *this meter's number on this flower*. A different meter, even the same model, moves the band without telling you.
5. Confirm the room is fully dark and the blackout is intact. Lights stay off for the entire cycle.
6. Set Phase 1 targets and let the room stabilize before hanging.
7. Hang at consistent spacing. Branches must not touch. Keep airflow paths open — dense corners are where mold starts. **On a Heavy load, resist the temptation to pack tighter; a crowded room dries slower, which is the opposite of what a heavy batch needs.**
8. **Tag three branches and record their harvest day wet weight.** Place them deliberately: one high in the room, one low, and one in the slowest-moving air — a corner away from the fans. These are the batch's weight-loss reference and the only non-destructive readiness measure available.

## 9. Environment Schedule

| Phase | Days | Weekday | Temp | RH | Dehus | Air exchange | Lights |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Initial drying** | 1–3 | Mon–Wed | 63°F | 20% | 20% | Intake + exhaust, continuous | Off |
| **2 — Controlled drying** | 4–7 | Thu–Sun | 65°F | 45% | 45% | 24 hours a day | Off |
| **3 — Hold & takedown** | 8 | Mon | 70°F | 55% | 55% | 24 hours a day | Off |
| **E — Extension** | 9–12 | Tue–Fri | 65°F | 45% | 45% | 24 hours a day | Off |

**Extension holds Phase 2 conditions. It does not continue Phase 3.** Phase 3 is a takedown-day hold — its job is to stop the flower drying while the crew works. Applying it to a batch that still needs to lose water stalls the dry and burns a day. If the Day 8 gate fails, the room goes back to 65°F / 45% the same morning.

**No fan points at hanging flower.** Fans move air through the room; they do not blow on plants. Some facing up, some pushing across. Direct airflow dries the exterior faster than moisture can migrate out of the interior.

**Phase 2 is the real control lever.** Days 4–7 are where a standard batch lands, and Days 9–12 are Phase 2 conditions extended. Phase 3 is one hold day. Treat Phase 2 setpoints accordingly.

## 10. Daily Checks — every day, Days 1 through takedown

- Confirm the TrolMaster setpoint and each portable dehumidifier setpoint match the phase table.
- Compare the actual room reading to the setpoint. **Drift over 2°F or over 5% RH gets corrected and noted.**
- **Record actual RH alongside setpoint every day.** On an extended cycle this record is the diagnosis (see §11).
- Verify dehumidifier drains are clear and reservoirs are not backed up.
- Confirm all fans are running and none has been bumped into pointing at the plants.
- Walk the room and scout for mold and botrytis. Anything suspect is removed and isolated immediately. **Twice daily from Day 8 on.**
- Confirm lights off and door sealed.
- Record temp, RH, and observations.

## 11. Decision Points

### Day 5 (Friday) — checkpoint · mandatory

The last day a change can meaningfully move the Day 8 result. It does not get skipped, and the Lead is notified either way — including when no action was needed.

1. **Reweigh all three tagged branches.** Calculate percent loss from their harvest day wet weight.
2. **Take pin meter readings.** Probe through the bud shoulder toward the stem with the pins fully sunk. Average 3–5 buds per point, across at least four positions in the room. Second reading on a thin twig at each point. Wipe pins with 70% IPA between samples.

| Finding | Reading | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Ahead of pace** | Weight loss over 75%, or buds at or below 12% MC | Raise RH toward 55% early to slow the dry. Notify Lead. |
| **On pace** | Weight loss around 55–70%, buds still above 14% MC | Hold Phase 2 setpoints. Record and notify. |
| **Behind pace** | Weight loss under 50%, or buds above 18% MC | Drop RH 5–10% for Days 6–7. Notify Lead. **Flag the batch as likely to extend** and tell the trim room. |

### Day 7 (Sunday) — routing decision

Run a short read: one tagged-branch weight and one meter sample. This decides what the room does overnight.

| Read | Route | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Tracking to pass — weight loss 70%+, buds approaching 12–13% | **Standard** | Set Phase 3 (70°F / 55%) Sunday evening so the room is stable Monday morning. |
| Clearly short — weight loss under 65%, buds above 14% | **Extension** | **Hold Phase 2 conditions.** Do not set Phase 3. Notify the Lead and the trim room that Monday takedown is at risk. |

Setting Phase 3 on a batch that then fails the Monday gate costs a day of drying at 55%. The Sunday read is what prevents that.

### Day 8 (Monday) — readiness gate

All three checks are taken and recorded. **From Day 8 on, this gate runs daily** until the batch passes or reaches Day 12 and escalates.

| Check | Criterion | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Snap test** | 60%+ of small lateral branches snap cleanly | Pencil-thickness and under. Main stalks still bending is normal — do not wait on them or you will overdry the batch. |
| **Moisture meter** | Buds average **11–12% MC**, thin stems 10–12% | Sample from at least four positions in the room, not one convenient rack. |
| **Harvest day wet weight** | **75–80% loss** from harvest day wet weight | The only check that catches a batch whose stems snap while the interior is still wet. |

**When the checks disagree:**

- **Weight wins on the wet side.** Dense buds can pass the snap test while holding water in the interior. If stems snap but weight loss is only around 60%, the snap is misleading.
- **The meter wins on the dry side.** Anything under 10% MC is overdried regardless of what the weight says. Get it down and flag it downstream — the cure cannot add moisture back.

**Photograph each meter reading in place.** One frame per room position, taken with the pins still sunk in the bud, so the reading, the position, and the probe depth all sit in the same picture. File the four frames with the batch record. This is the cheapest quality reference the room produces: over a few cycles it builds a visual library of what 11–12% actually looks like, which is something a number cannot teach the trim crew, and it gives the Day 5 and Day 7 pace bands something to check against when they are replaced with real data.

### Days 9–12 — extension management

Each extension day requires the Lead's authorization, recorded on the log with the reason. Extension is not automatic and it is not open-ended.

**Every extension day:**

1. Confirm the room went back to Phase 2 conditions (65°F / 45%) — not Phase 3.
2. Re-run the full three-check gate.
3. **Compare actual RH against setpoint across the whole cycle.** This is the diagnostic that matters:

| Pattern | Diagnosis | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Actual RH tracked setpoint all cycle | The load was simply large. The dry is working, it just needs time. | Continue the extension. Nothing to fix. |
| **Actual RH ran consistently above setpoint** | **Dehumidification capacity is the constraint, not the setpoint.** Turning the dial lower will do nothing — the room already cannot reach the number it has. | Add portable units, reduce fresh-air exchange rate, check for a failed or iced dehumidifier, verify AC is holding. Escalate to the Lead the same day. |
| Actual RH tracked setpoint but temperature ran high | AC control is marginal. Warm air holds more water and the dehus fight it. | Escalate. Note for the load assessment on future cycles. |

4. Scout for mold twice. Extended time at moderate humidity is the botrytis window.

### Day 12 (Friday) — mandatory escalation

**Day 12 does not force the batch down.** Standing policy is that flower does not come down until it is ready, and a wet batch bucked to meet a date is a worse outcome than a longer hang. What Day 12 forces is that the decision gets made deliberately, by the Lead and the Supervisor together, and written down. The date cannot pass quietly.

**On Day 12, whether or not the batch passes:**

1. Run the full three-check gate and record every result.
2. **The Cultivation Lead escalates to the Supervisor (Jeff) the same day.** This is not optional and it does not wait for Monday.
3. Lead and Supervisor decide together, and the decision goes on the log with its cause:
   - **Take down** — the batch passes, or is close enough that the cure can finish it. Flag it per the exception table.
   - **Keep hanging** — with mold scouting twice daily, a stated re-check date, and the room-availability conflict resolved before the next harvest is cut.
4. **The probable cause is documented either way** — load size, dehumidification capacity, AC control, hang density — so the next load assessment is better calibrated.
5. The trim room lead and Rob are told the same day, not on the new takedown morning.

**What has to be weighed.** Mold risk climbs with every additional day at moderate humidity; the room is needed for Monday's harvest; and the downstream schedule has already slipped. None of those override readiness — that is the point of the standing policy — but all three are why the call is escalated rather than left to run on its own.

## 12. Exceptions at Takedown

| Condition | Response |
|---|---|
| **Checks pass** | Buck on schedule. Record readings on the batch record. Cure length judged normally by Rob. |
| **Wet but workable** — stems snap, weight loss 65–75%, buds 12–14% | Buck. **Flag the lot for a full 3-day cure** and note it on the batch record. Lids off the full 15–20 minutes every morning. |
| **Clearly not ready, before Day 12** — weight loss under 65%, buds over 15% | Do not buck. Extend one day with the Lead's authorization. Re-run the gate tomorrow. |
| **Clearly not ready, at Day 12** | **Do not buck on the date alone.** Run the full gate, escalate to the Lead and the Supervisor the same day, and record their decision with its cause. If the decision is to take down: extended cure, verbal handoff, closing readings on the batch record. If the decision is to keep hanging: twice-daily mold scouting, a stated re-check date, and the room conflict resolved before the next harvest is cut. |
| **Overdried** — buds under 10% MC, weight loss over 82% | Buck on schedule and **notify post-processing**. The cure cannot put moisture back. Overdried flower sheds trichomes in machine trim and breaks up on the sifting tray — handling gets gentled downstream, and the cause gets investigated before the next cycle. |

## 13. Handoff to Post-Processing

Takedown feeds directly into Stage 1 of the Post-Processing SOP.

- Flower comes down and goes **immediately into the trim room**. It does not sit in the buck room, a hallway, or on the dry room floor after take-down.
- Bucking happens in the trim room at 58–62°F / 55–60% RH. The buck room cannot hold that target; the trim room can.
- 27 gal tote, **6–7 lb max** during cure. Do not begin bucking until the tote is full.
- **Pass the takedown-day readings forward on the batch record.** Rob judges cure length by moisture — the entry moisture is the starting point for that judgment, and he should not have to guess at it.
- **On any extended cycle, tell the trim room lead verbally as well as on paper**, and tell them as early as the Day 5 or Day 7 read, not on the takedown day.

## 15. Troubleshooting

- **Room will not hold RH** — check dehumidifier drains, filters, and door seal. If the fresh air intake is bringing in wet air, reduce the exchange rate and notify the Lead. Do not chase it with temperature.
- **Actual RH consistently above setpoint** — capacity, not setpoint. Lowering the dial will not help. Add portables, cut exchange rate, check for an iced or failed unit, verify AC. This is the single most common cause of a batch needing extension.
- **TrolMaster disagrees with the orange handheld** — recalibrate the sensor. Do not compensate by changing setpoints; you will be flying blind for the rest of the cycle.
- **Mold or botrytis found** — remove and isolate immediately. Do not carry it through the room. Notify the Lead. Increase airflow; do not raise temperature.
- **Crispy exterior, soft interior (case hardening)** — the dry is outrunning moisture migration. Raise RH immediately and reduce fan speed. Note it on the batch record and plan for a longer cure.
- **Meter readings scattered widely across the room** — an airflow or hang-density problem, not a meter problem. Reposition fans, open up crowded sections, note the affected positions.
- **Tagged branches disagree by more than about 10%** — the room is drying unevenly. Record which positions are fast and slow, and correct fan placement before the next cycle.
- **Batch extends past Day 10 with no clear cause** — pull the whole cycle's setpoint-vs-actual record before changing anything. The answer is almost always in that column.

## 16. Changes from the Previous SOP

Called out so they can be reviewed rather than absorbed silently.

- **Day 8 target with a Day 12 outer bound**, replacing the open-ended "Day 8–12 onward." Extension is now a defined state with entry criteria, daily authorization, and a mandatory escalation at the outer bound.
- **Day 12 is a mandatory escalation, not a hard stop.** An earlier draft of this SOP forced the batch down on Day 12 regardless of readings. That contradicts standing policy — flower does not come down until it is ready. Day 12 now forces a documented decision by the Lead and the Supervisor instead of a takedown. The discipline is unchanged: the date cannot pass without a recorded decision and a recorded cause.
- **The moisture meter is named and pinned to the room.** The SOP previously said only "pin-type moisture meter." Because no pin meter carries a cannabis calibration — they all read a wood scale — the acceptance band is only meaningful against one specific unit. The room's dedicated FLIR MR40 is now named in the equipment list, and Room Prep forbids substituting another meter mid-cycle.
- **Photo step added to the readiness gate**, one frame per room position with the pins still in the bud.
- **Load assessment at intake added**, classifying each batch Standard or Heavy before the cycle starts.
- **Day 5 checkpoint added** for pace correction.
- **Day 7 routing decision added**, so Phase 3 is not set on a batch that will fail the Monday gate.
- **Terminology: "sentinel" replaced with "harvest day wet weight."** The old word meant nothing to anyone reading it cold. The three branches are now just the tagged branches, and the measurement is stated as percent loss from harvest day wet weight.
- **Sealed-container equilibration removed.** It was added in v1.2 and taken back out — the hour-plus wait before a usable read does not fit the takedown-morning shift. Interior moisture is now covered only indirectly, by weight loss. See Open Items.
- **Harvest day wet weight tracking added.** Non-destructive, in-place, and the only check that catches a batch whose stems snap while the interior is still wet.
- **Setpoint-vs-actual RH recorded daily**, and used as the extension diagnostic.
- **Moisture acceptance narrowed from 8–12% to 11–12%.** 8% is overdried flower — brittle, trichome-shedding, and lighter on the scale. The previous range let an overdried lot pass verification.
- **Lights-off requirement stated explicitly.** The previous SOP did not mention darkness at all.
- **"No fan pointed at plants"** stated explicitly.
- **Exception table added.** The previous SOP defined pass criteria but not what to do on a fail.
- **Explicit handoff to Post-Processing Stage 1**, including passing takedown readings forward and giving early verbal warning on extended cycles.
- Snap test clarified to **small lateral branches, pencil-thickness and under**.

## 17. Open Items

*Tracked here, not published as placeholders.*

- **Room availability during an extension is unresolved.** Harvest is weekly and the dry room is single. Days 9–12 of one batch overlap Days 1–5 of the next. The SOP flags this at intake but does not solve it — the options are a second dry space, staging the next harvest, or reducing/splitting a harvest when the prior batch is classified Heavy. Needs a decision before it happens on a real Monday.
- **The Days 1–3 setpoint of 20% RH is the biggest open question in this SOP.** At 20% the bud exterior can dry far faster than moisture migrates out of the interior, which crusts the surface, traps water inside, and locks chlorophyll into the tissue — the classic path to a hay smell and a batch that reads dry outside and wet in the core. Common commercial practice runs 55–60% RH throughout, or steps *down* rather than up. Worth running one room at 50% for Days 1–3 alongside a 20% room and comparing Day 5 branch weights, finished aroma, and machine-trim breakage.
- **Phase 3 at 70°F** sits at the top of the accepted band; 60–68°F is the more common range, and 70°F accelerates terpene volatilization. Consider capping at 68°F.
- **The Day 5 and Day 7 pace bands are estimates, not measured.** The first two or three cycles of branch weights will establish NLT's actual curve. Replace them with real numbers once there is data.
- **Load assessment thresholds are qualitative.** Once a few cycles of poundage-vs-days-to-ready are logged, "Heavy" can be defined by an actual number of pounds per dehumidifier rather than by judgment.
- **Only one moisture meter on site, so there is no drift check.** A second matched MR40 would let each cycle's readings be cross-checked between two units, which is the cheapest way to catch a meter going bad. A drifting meter is dangerous precisely because it reads plausibly — it will quietly mis-set the acceptance band for every batch until somebody notices. Until there is a second unit, the harvest day wet weight is the only independent check standing between a drifted meter and a mis-dried harvest. A loss-on-drying analyzer (Ohaus MB32, roughly $3,100) would anchor the meter to true moisture content; the water activity question below is separate and more urgent.
- **No water activity instrument on site.** The pin meter reads moisture content, which steers the dry but does not predict microbial risk. A real instrument would confirm the pin meter has not drifted and give a defensible release number. Shortlisted: Rotronic AwEasy (~$4,600, ±0.008 aw, Bluetooth with per-batch PDF reports); METER AQUALAB 3 is the chilled-mirror reference.
- **Nothing on site reads interior moisture directly.** The snap test reads the stem, the meter reads near the bud surface, and weight loss reads the whole branch — none of them sees a wet core behind a dry shell. Weight loss is the closest proxy and the reason it outranks the snap test on the wet side. A water activity meter is the real answer; a sealed-container check was trialled in v1.2 and dropped for taking too long on a takedown morning.
- **No drying-rate target defined.** Roughly 1% moisture loss per day is a common benchmark; worth checking the weight-loss curve against it once there is a baseline.
